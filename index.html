Scientific Context: Mechanoregulation Laws

Comparing Notermans et al. (2021) model hypotheses for cellular response to load

Biophysical Theory

Option 1 (Plateau)

Assumes collagen synthesis increases with strain and simply plateaus at high levels. It predicts unhindered healing even under extreme loads.

Option 2 (Inhibition) • Core Hypothesis

The paper's core hypothesis. It models how excessive mechanical strain (>15%) physically damages cells and downregulates collagen synthesis, successfully predicting the "core healing defect" seen in aggressive real-world rehabilitation.

Educational Insights

Core vs. Periphery
Tendon Core Callus High Strain / Delay

Central tissue sustains concentrated axial deformation. Under Option 2 (strain-inhibited), strains exceeding physiological bounds shut down fibroblast synthesis, reproducing the central healing delay documented by Notermans et al. (2021).

Tendon Periphery Collar Optimal Window

The bulging outer collar operates within the ideal mechanotransductive strain window (approx. 4–8%), triggering intense collagen deposition and rapid longitudinal fibril steering to bridge load transfer.

About the Model

Notermans et al. (2021)

Computational Multiscale Mechanobiology of Achilles Tendon Healing

Overview

This application simulates the multiscale mechanobiology of Achilles tendon healing based on the computational model developed by Notermans et al. (2021).

The Clinical Problem

Aggressive early rehabilitation often leads to a central 'core healing defect' within the tendon, while the periphery heals normally. This simulation models how mechanical strain dictates cellular behavior to explain this phenomenon.

Core Scientific Findings
